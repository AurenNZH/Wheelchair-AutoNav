2026-08-11T16:30:00+01:00 — Acquisition-age latency gate

The first protocol-v2 shadow capture classified all joystick directions and
transported them correctly, but every motion authorization stopped. The
supervisor observed a 386 ms median front-map age, 687 ms p95, and 1332 ms
maximum against its 300 ms fail-closed limit. Four stale-map envelopes caused
the later automatic-stop latch messages; CLEAR and SLOW were never exercised.

The safety meaning of age remains the original AIRY acquisition timestamp.
Output restamping or a larger supervisor limit would conceal old evidence, so
neither is used. The 300 ms deadline remains fixed and the engineering target
is p99 at or below 250 ms, leaving 50 ms margin.

The mapper formerly published a full local grid, then the front grid, then ran
and published the artifact shadow and its RViz products in one callback. The
safety profile now prioritizes and publishes the front grid, skips unused
surround and artifact outputs, uses a latest-only AIRY queue, and displays only
the safety map at 10 FPS. Full artifact calibration remains available under a
separate explicit debug profile and is not an enforcement configuration.

Performance acceptance uses the normal Jetson MODE_20W_6CORE profile without
pinning maximum clocks. Measurements separate source age, callback-entry age,
front publication age, supervisor receipt age, processing stages, map gaps,
and system utilization so GPU load is demonstrated by A/B evidence rather
than assumed.

2026-08-12T18:01:02+01:00 — Runtime profile parameter authority

The first headless safety capture still performed local-grid and artifact
debug work even though the top-level launch requested both switches false.
ROS Foxy applied the node-specific true values from local_mapping.yaml over
the wildcard launch parameter file. Artifact filtering and publication alone
averaged about 97 ms per cloud, reducing the observed map rate to 6.42 Hz.

The two output switches are therefore launch-owned and removed from the
node-specific mapping YAML. Direct local-mapping launches retain full-debug
defaults, while the top-level safety and artifact_debug profiles explicitly
select false/false and true/true respectively. Runtime validation checks the
effective node parameters before measuring latency.

2026-08-12T22:37:14+01:00 — Reliable latest-only LiDAR delivery

The AIRY source, Ethernet input, and RoboSense core produced complete clouds
at 10 Hz. SDK instrumentation measured about 1.6 ms to convert each 1.38 MB
cloud and 1.6 ms to publish it, with assembled-cloud gaps below 103 ms. A
minimal best-effort ROS subscriber received only 7.4 Hz and saw gaps up to
1402 ms, while an otherwise identical reliable subscriber received 10.0 Hz
without a gap over 160 ms. Large fragmented samples were therefore being lost
between the reliable SDK publisher and the mapper's best-effort reader.

The production mapper now uses reliable, volatile, keep-last depth one. Depth
one preserves latest-only processing while DDS reliability repairs missing
fragments instead of discarding a whole cloud. A three-minute temporary A/B
run of this contract delivered 1803 front maps with 198.1 ms p99 age, 236.6 ms
maximum age, and zero supervisor stale-map events. Acquisition timestamps and
the 300 ms fail-closed deadline remain unchanged.

Latency acceptance now fails on any map-arrival gap over 300 ms, any increase
in the supervisor's stale receipt/event counters during measurement, or
missing, malformed, or reset supervisor diagnostics. Warm-up counter values
form the baseline so startup transitions are not misclassified as capture
failures. This prevents a low received-map age from hiding dropped intervals.

2026-08-12T23:15:28+01:00 — Production acceptance

The installed production path passed both five-minute non-actuating captures.
Headless safety delivered 2994 maps at 9.97 Hz with 213.4 ms p99 age and
268.2 ms maximum age. The lightweight RViz safety view delivered 2989 maps at
9.95 Hz with 187.4 ms p99 age and 241.2 ms maximum age. Both captures had zero

and valid supervisor diagnostics. RViz reduced measured rate by about 0.2%
and did not increase p99 age, satisfying the visualization overhead gate.

The recorder now also requires its measured map rate to remain at or above
9 Hz. This closes the last case where a uniformly slow stream could avoid the
300 ms gap threshold yet still report a misleading pass.
